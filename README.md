# SpEd Agent

**Every California IEP meeting on time, without the chase.**

SpEd Agent is a prototype for special education coordinators. It reads the spreadsheets they already have, works out every IEP deadline the way California law counts days, and shows who is overdue or close. Then it books the meeting: it finds times when the whole IEP team is free, writes to the family in their language, and holds the time on everyone's calendar. Nothing reaches a family until the coordinator approves it.

**[Try the live demo →](https://hdronamraju.github.io/sped-agent/)** No sign-in. It uses a fictional district, and nothing is sent anywhere.

> **Prototype, not legal advice.** The deadline rules come from a product spec and general knowledge of California and federal special education law. They have **not** been reviewed by a California special-ed attorney or SELPA, or checked against the current official text of the law. Every student, staff member and calendar in the demo is made up. Please don't enter real student information.

## Try the demo in 5 minutes

1. Open the [demo](https://hdronamraju.github.io/sped-agent/) and click **Try it with sample data**. You land on the at-risk list. Click any row to see how its date was counted.
2. Go to **Today** → "Hold initial IEP meeting · Sofia Hernandez" → **Schedule**.
3. Click **Find 3 times**, read the message to her mom in Spanish, then **Approve & send**. In the demo it goes to a pretend outbox.
4. Type a reply like `La 2 por favor` and click **Simulate reply**. The agent holds the time and drafts the formal meeting notice. Approve it.
5. Click **Demo: … declines** to see it find a new time that still meets the deadline.
6. Open **Audit log** → **Verify integrity**.

The sample district is set in fall 2026. To try another date, use **Settings → Pretend today is**.

## How it works

- **It runs in the browser.** There's no server and no account. Anything a coordinator uploads stays on their own computer.
- **Deadlines:** it reads a SEIS export or tracker (CSV or Excel, any column names) and the school calendar (PDF, .ics or link). Then it calculates the six California timelines, skipping summer and school breaks longer than 5 school days. Every date shows its working and the law it comes from.
- **Scheduling:**
  - It lists the required IEP team members and adds an interpreter when the family's language isn't English.
  - It finds three times on different days, all before the legal deadline. It never proposes a time past the deadline; if none fits, it escalates to the director.
  - It drafts the meeting notice in the family's language and checks it has everything the law requires.
- **Proof:** every upload, calculation, approval and message goes into an audit log that shows if anyone edits history.
- **Calendars and email:** it can connect to Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 with the coordinator's own sign-in. The demo uses pretend calendars instead.

### Known limits

- **No server, by design.** Data lives in the coordinator's browser (IndexedDB), so it doesn't sync between people or devices. Roles are per device, not real access control. A shared backend is the natural next step once a district signs the CA-NDPA.
- **Texting parents** isn't wired in. Parents are reached by email. `workers/twilio-sms/` has a small Worker to start from.
- **Morning digest** sends the first time the app is opened each day (there's nothing to run on a schedule), or from a button.
- **District notice templates**: the app uses built-in English and Spanish notices. Swapping in a district's own template is not built yet. Other languages need a translation (optional AI draft, or the district's translator) before approval.
- **SEIS** is read-only, by file upload. The nightly feed (P2-1) isn't built.

## California rules and interpretations to review

The rules are data in `src/rules/ca-rules.ts` (version `CA-2026.10-r1`). A law change is a data edit plus a test case. **Before a pilot, a California special-ed attorney or SELPA should review these readings.** Where the law could be read more than one way, we chose the reading that gives the earlier date:

1. **Day counting.** Calendar days, skipping days between regular sessions and vacations of **more than 5 school days**. A qualifying vacation skips only its declared dates; weekends just before or after it still count. Holidays touching a break (even across a weekend) join it: Cesar Chavez Day + spring break = 6 school days, so it's skipped.
2. **Start day is day 0.** A referral on Oct 5 has a 15-day plan due Oct 20.
3. **Assessment plan year-end rule** (EC 56321(a)): a referral 10 or fewer calendar days before the last day of school is due on day 10 of the next school year (first day = day 1).
4. **IEP year-end rule** (EC 56344(a)): a referral 30 or fewer days before year end is due on day 30 of the next school year **or** by the normal 60-day count, whichever is earlier. This only applies if consent arrives before the new year starts.
5. **Annual and triennial** are due the day before the anniversary (Feb 29 → Feb 28).
6. **Act-by date.** If a due date falls on a weekend, holiday or summer, the queue uses the last school day before it.
7. **Triennial start.** Send the assessment plan 75 counted days before the 3-year date (60-day assessment + 15-day parent review).
8. **SEIS cross-check.** If SEIS shows an earlier due date than ours, we use SEIS's and flag the difference.
9. **Missing next-year calendar.** The summer gap is estimated and the date is marked *estimated* until next year's calendar is uploaded.

Citations: EC 56321(a), 56321(c)(4), 56344(a), 56043(l), 56043(d), 56341, 56341.1(g), 56341.5; 34 CFR 300.303, 300.321, 300.322, 300.324.

## Connecting Google or Microsoft

Everything runs in the browser with the coordinator's own sign-in, using the PKCE / token-client flows, so there's no client secret. IT creates an app registration; the coordinator pastes the **ID** (not a secret) into **Settings → Connections**. The in-app IT packet has these steps pre-filled for the detected platform.

**Google Workspace**
1. Google Cloud console → enable **Google Calendar API** and **Gmail API**.
2. OAuth consent screen → **Internal**.
3. Credentials → OAuth client ID → **Web application** → Authorized JavaScript origin = where the app is hosted (e.g. `http://localhost:5173` for dev).
4. Scopes requested: `calendar.freebusy`, `calendar.events`, `gmail.send`, `gmail.readonly` (only to read replies in threads the agent started).

**Microsoft 365**
1. Entra admin center → App registrations → New (this organization only).
2. Authentication → **Single-page application** → redirect URI = the app URL.
3. Delegated Graph permissions: `User.Read`, `Calendars.ReadWrite`, `Calendars.Read.Shared`, `Mail.Send`, `Mail.ReadWrite` → grant admin consent.

Tokens are kept in memory or session storage only; after a reload the coordinator reconnects.

## Optional AI helpers

Off by default. With an Anthropic API key (Settings → AI helpers), Claude can:
- suggest mappings for columns the rules didn't recognize (only headers and 3 sample values are sent);
- re-read a calendar PDF the rules-based reader struggled with (only calendar text is sent);
- draft a translation of a notice into languages without a built-in template (a person reviews it before approval).

Every helper has a non-AI path. Student records are never sent.

```
  integrations/ provider interface, google, microsoft, demo, mime
  audit/        hash-chained log, PDF/CSV export
  itPacket/     IT request PDF + email
  ai/           optional Claude helpers
  store/        app state (zustand + IndexedDB) and the agent workflow
  ui/           pages and components
tests/          rules (62 hand-calculated cases), ingest, agent, providers, e2e
public/samples/ fictional sample district (regenerate with `npm run samples`)
workers/        optional Twilio SMS worker
```
