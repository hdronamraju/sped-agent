# What non-compliance costs: legal, financial, learning

**As of 2026-10-13.** Every claim here links to a page opened during research.

## Legal

A California district that misses IEP obligations faces four tracks at once: OAH due process, CDE state complaints, federal disability discrimination suits, and federal monitoring of the state. Two Supreme Court rulings since 2023 make the lawsuit track easier for families.

**The standard**
- An IEP must be "appropriately ambitious" in light of the child's circumstances, markedly more demanding than "merely more than de minimis" ([Endrew F., 2017](https://law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/15-827)).
- A procedural lapse is a denial of FAPE when it impeded the child's right to FAPE, significantly impeded parents' participation, or caused a loss of educational benefit; a hearing officer can order procedural compliance regardless ([20 U.S.C. 1415(f)(3)(E)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)).

**Due process (OAH)**
- Two-year filing window in California ([EC 56505(l), per OAH decision](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/-/media/Divisions/OAH/Special-Education/SE-Decisions/2025/10---Oct/2025070683-AccMod.pdf)). It doesn't apply if the district misrepresented that it fixed the problem or withheld required information ([20 U.S.C. 1415(f)(3)(D)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)). Missing notices can extend exposure.
- Courts grant the relief they find appropriate ([20 U.S.C. 1415(i)(2)(C)(iii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)): compensatory education and reimbursement, limited in California to the two-year window ([Disability Rights California](https://serr.disabilityrightsca.org/?p=1731)).
- Private placement tuition can be reimbursed when the district denied FAPE and the placement was proper ([Florence County v. Carter, 1993](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/510/7)).

**State complaints (CDE)**
- Anyone can file; violation within 1 year; decision in 60 days. Corrective actions can include assessments, training, compensatory education or reimbursement, revised procedures, monitoring, and a new IEP meeting. Refusing to cooperate can produce a finding on the evidence CDE has. Investigators review service logs, assessments, IEPs, meeting notes and Prior Written Notices ([CDE complaint process](https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/se/cmplntproc.asp)).

**ADA and Section 504 suits**
- Families seeking damages under the ADA don't have to exhaust IDEA due process first ([Perez v. Sturgis, 2023](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/us/598/21-887/)).
- Students no longer have to prove "bad faith or gross misjudgment" ([A.J.T. v. Osseo, June 12, 2025](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/6/supreme-court-decides-ajt-v-osseo-area-schools-independent-school-district-no-279)); district-side counsel read this as making damages easier to win ([Bond Schoeneck & King](https://www.bsk.com/news-events-videos/supreme-court-issues-decision-on-legal-standard-for-students-claiming-disability-discrimination-under-section-504)).

**Discipline**
- If a parent expressed concern in writing or asked for an evaluation before the misconduct, the district is deemed to know about the disability and IDEA discipline protections apply ([20 U.S.C. 1415(k)(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)).
- Misconduct caused by the district's failure to implement the IEP is a manifestation by definition ([20 U.S.C. 1415(k)(1)(E)–(F)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)).

**Federal monitoring**
- California is "needs assistance" under IDEA Part B for two or more consecutive years in the June 2026 determinations; that level requires federal enforcement action such as technical assistance, high-risk grantee status or directed set-aside funds ([ED 2026 fact sheet](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/files/ideafactsheet-determinations-2026.pdf)).
- States must rate each district on the same four levels and escalate enforcement ([NCSI/OSEP, March 2025](https://ncsi.wested.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/NCSI-Gen-Sup-TA-Priority-Mar-19-2025.pdf)).

## Financial

The largest cost of a lost case can be the family's attorney fees rather than the service itself, and those awards often come from the district's general fund. Reporting in 2021 put the gap at a few thousand dollars for services against fee awards that can top $100,000 ([EdSource, 2021](https://edsource.org/2021/how-california-plans-to-deter-costly-special-education-disputes/658226?amp=1)).

| Cost | Legal basis | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family's attorney fees | Reasonable fees to prevailing parents, community rates, no multiplier ([20 U.S.C. 1415(i)(3)(B)–(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)) | California rates rising; awards often paid from general funds; one LAUSD case requested nearly $875,000 ([CSBA ELA, 2025](https://publications.csba.org/reports/ela/2025-annual-report/special-education-standard-for-fape/)) |
| District's own legal costs | Recoverable only if the parent's case was frivolous or improper ([1415(i)(3)(B)(i)(II)–(III)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)) | No statewide figure found |
| Private tuition | Reimbursement when FAPE denied and placement proper ([Carter](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/510/7)) | Case by case |
| Compensatory education / reimbursement | OAH order or CDE corrective action ([CDE](https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/se/cmplntproc.asp)) | Two-year limit ([DRC](https://serr.disabilityrightsca.org/?p=1731)) |
| Money damages | ADA without IDEA exhaustion ([Perez](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/us/598/21-887/)); no heightened intent bar ([A.J.T.](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/6/supreme-court-decides-ajt-v-osseo-area-schools-independent-school-district-no-279)) | Too recent for CA totals |
| Federal funds | "Needs intervention" 3+ years: withhold 20–50% of certain state-level funds; "substantial intervention": withhold further payments ([20 U.S.C. 1416(e)](https://www.customsmobile.com/uscode/title20_chapter33_subchapterII_section1416)); states can direct, recover or withhold district funds ([NCSI/OSEP](https://ncsi.wested.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/NCSI-Gen-Sup-TA-Priority-Mar-19-2025.pdf)) | CA is "needs assistance," not intervention, for Part B in 2026 ([ED](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/files/ideafactsheet-determinations-2026.pdf)) |
| Significant disproportionality | Must reserve 15% of IDEA funds for early intervening services ([CDE letter via El Dorado Charter SELPA](https://charterselpa.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Annotated-Annual-Determination-Letter-with-Sample-Data-Sheet-for-23.24-Monitoring.pdf)) | Named list published by CDE (see [05](05-public-data-sources.md)) |

**District levers:** a written settlement offer more than 10 days before a hearing caps recoverable fees if the result is no better; no fees for ordinary IEP meetings ([20 U.S.C. 1415(i)(3)(D)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)).

**California context:** nearly $24 billion went to students with disabilities in 2024–25, just over 17% of school spending; districts covered 71% of costs in 2025, up from 63% in 2015 ([PPIC, Oct 2026](https://www.ppic.org/wp-content/uploads/students-with-disabilities-in-california.pdf)). California sees more disputes than most states and set aside $100 million in 2021 to resolve them outside litigation ([EdSource, 2021](https://edsource.org/2021/how-california-plans-to-deter-costly-special-education-disputes/658226?amp=1)).

## Learning

Reading research shows the same intervention works less well when it starts later, so missed timelines can cost gains students don't fully recover. Federal law treats lost learning as the harm that turns a procedural lapse into a FAPE denial ([20 U.S.C. 1415(f)(3)(E)(ii)(III)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)).

- The same intervention in 1st or 2nd grade produced almost twice the word-reading gains of 3rd grade, and 1st graders kept growing faster afterward ([Lovett et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9164258)).
- Kindergartners who started in September did significantly better than those who started mid-February; twice as many reached the average range ([O'Connor et al., 2010](https://edknowledge.stanford.edu/index.php/node/213)).
- Intensive early reading interventions: mean effect size 0.39 (0.28 bias-adjusted); one-on-one 0.59 vs groups 0.33 ([Wanzek et al., 2018](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/958d247e-242e-4422-8f71-710e330be8ed)).
- `Hypothesis`: comparable timing effects for behavior or autism supports weren't reviewed.

Unimplemented services are lost instruction: the student in A.J.T. got about 65% of her peers' instructional time ([CEC](https://exceptionalchildren.org/blog/supreme-court-affirms-rights-student-disabilities)); Endrew F. began with progress that stalled under repeated IEPs ([Endrew F.](https://law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/15-827)). Students removed more than 10 school days must keep receiving services ([20 U.S.C. 1415(k)(1)(D)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1415)).
